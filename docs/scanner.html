Note: the Lab explores the old PRO4 reversal model. The Connors RSI-2 strategy was found and validated separately in a larger offline study, so use the Backtest tab for it.
The Lab tests 7,776 versions of the strategy on your downloaded history. The variations cover: 6 sessions (Asia, London, NY open, NY afternoon, all day, your pair sessions); 3 RSI levels (20/80, 25/75, 30/70); 3 Bollinger widths (2.0, 2.2, 2.5); 3 Stochastic settings (zone 20, zone 30, off); 4 regime filters (none, range-only ADX<25 or <20, trade with the EMA200 trend); 4 exit styles (your fixed pips, two ATR-based exits, and the classic mean-reversion target of the middle band); and 3 breakeven rules.

To avoid fooling ourselves, each version is ranked only on the older part of the history (in-sample), then shown how it did on the newer part it never saw (out-of-sample). All four pairs share one set of rules, which makes the results much harder to fake than tuning each pair separately. If nothing holds up out-of-sample, the honest answer is that this entry pattern doesn't have an edge here.

Active strategy: Connors RSI-2 on the hourly chart. This is Larry Connors' published pullback system. It buys short, sharp dips in a strong uptrend and sells short spikes in a strong downtrend. A signal needs four things at an hourly close between 07:00 and 16:00 NY: (1) price on the right side of the 200-hour average, (2) RSI(2) below 5 for buys or above 95 for sells, (3) ADX above 25, meaning the trend is strong, and (4) the target being at least 3 pips away. The take-profit is 75% of the way back to the 5-hour average. The stop is 2× the hourly ATR. Set both in MT4 when the alert arrives and leave the trade alone.

In offline testing on 9 months of data, with spread plus $10/lot commission, it made 67 trades with about 88% winners and +8 pips per trade. It was positive in all 5 time slices and on all 4 pairs. Out of 16 well-known strategies tested, it was the only one that beat random entries. That still isn't a guarantee: it averages about 1.7 trades a week, so paper trade it first.
TEST pairs: NZD/USD and EUR/CHF. These two came out of the Nov 26 deep dive. Each passed a check on locked data, but only on 8–9 trades, so they could still be luck. Their alerts say "TEST · paper only", and every signal is tracked in the journal. Their grade shows T while testing, along with live progress. After 15+ live paper trades with 80%+ wins and a profit, it switches to a letter grade (A/B/C), the label changes to PASSED TEST, and you get a phone alert. If one is under 70% wins or losing money after 10+ trades, it shows D · failing, and you should switch it off in Settings. Clearing the journal resets the test.
NZD/USD: H1 above the 200-hour average, RSI(2) below 15, Stochastic(14) in the bottom 15%, and a bullish 5-minute candle at the hourly close. TP = $3 net at 0.03 lot, SL = 4× the TP, close by hand after 24h. SELL is the mirror image.
EUR/CHF: H1 Stochastic(14,3,3) crosses up below 20 (or down above 80), ADX above 25, and Bollinger width in its top 30%. TP = 0.5× hourly ATR, SL = 2× ATR, close by hand after 72h.
Order of operations. (1) Paste your Twelve Data key in Settings. (2) Backtest tab: download 6–12 months of history (about 1 credit per 5,000 bars; the download respects the 8-calls-per-minute limit). (3) Run the Strategy Lab. (4) If a version is marked robust, open Details, then press Apply. (5) Run the Backtest so live signals get a grade. (6) Paper trade it for 3–4 weeks before you put money on it.
Phone alerts (Android). Install ntfy from the Play Store and subscribe to a hard-to-guess topic name. Enter the same name in Settings and press "Send test alert".
Keep it running. Leave this tab open in Chrome (minimized is fine) and stop the PC from sleeping. The scanner checks 15 seconds after every 5-minute close and only uses credits while a pair's session is open. An "All day" session on 4 pairs needs about 1,050 credits a day, which is more than the free 800, so the Settings page warns you about that.
How the backtest fills trades. Entry is at the close of the signal bar, with your spread added on buys. TP and SL are checked against bid for buys and ask for sells, the way MT5 does it. If one bar touches both SL and TP, it counts as a loss. Breakeven moves the stop to entry plus spread once the trade reaches the trigger.
Reading the Lab. R means profit or loss in units of the stop distance: +1R means you made what you risked. Avg R is your edge per trade. t-score measures how consistent that edge is; above about 2 means the result is unlikely to be luck. Because the Lab tests thousands of versions, a few will look good in-sample purely by chance. That's why out-of-sample is the column to trust. A version counts as robust only if it has a t-score of 2 or more in both halves, at least 30 out-of-sample trades, and an out-of-sample profit factor above 1.2. I calibrated that bar on purpose: on 8 sets of pure random price data, which has no edge by definition, nothing passed it. On synthetic data that really mean-reverts, most of the top versions passed. "~ lead" means positive on unseen data but not proven yet.
Data feed vs. your broker. Twelve Data is a composite feed, so a band touch here can miss at your broker by a fraction of a pip. Use the trade list to spot-check entries against your broker's chart, and set each pair's spread to what your broker actually charges.
